S03 / 02

Brand deconstruction: four layers

Apply these four layers to any brand and its photography starts explaining itself.

Core

What it stands for

Mission, values, origin story. Cocoon exists to prove Vietnamese botanicals belong in beauty. Liquid Death exists to make water rebellious.

Personality

Five adjectives

If the brand walked into the studio, how would it speak? Quiet, playful, precise, sensual, defiant. Write exactly five.

Visual codes

What it owns on sight

Colour, typography, materials, negative space, and the lighting language: hard or soft, warm or cool, deep or open shadows.

Context

Where it competes

Price point, competitors, cultural position. The same jar photographs differently at 200,000 VND and at 2,000,000 VND.

ApplyDeconstruct one of your pre-activity brands in four lines, one per layer. Five minutes, no browsing.

S03 / 04

Audience and the persona sentence

Demographics say who buys; psychographics say why. Then the question that actually changes the photograph: where will the image live? A product page, an Instagram Reel, a billboard on Nguyen Hue, and a package on a shelf each demand different framing, resolution, and negative space.

Before writing a shot list, complete this sentence for your brand:

This image is for [who], scrolling [where], feeling [what], and it should make them [do what].
S03 / 05

Building the moodboard

Rules
  • 6 to 9 frames, no more. Curation is the skill.
  • Mix real campaign references with AI mood frames.
  • Label every frame with what it contributes: palette, light, composition, or styling.
  • Include one texture close-up and one colour study.
  • Every frame must be buildable in our studio.
Colour story
  • Pull 3 to 5 colours from the brand's website and packaging.
  • Find the owned colour first: Hermes orange, Crumbl pink, Katinat teal.
  • High saturation reads fun and fast; low saturation reads calm and premium.
  • Colour temperature is a brand code: warm candlelit heritage or cool clinical precision.
  • Tools: Canva, Milanote, PureRef.

ApplyOpen your brand's homepage, pull its owned colour, and note the hex code as the first entry of your colour story.

S03 / 06

AI: craft first, then leverage

The ladder rule: every AI move below is locked to a fundamental you already practise in the studio. If you cannot do the craft on a rung, skip its AI move, because you will not be able to judge the output. AI proposes, the photographer disposes.

  1. Step 1

    The Gate: no AI yet

    CraftManual exposure, one-light control, brand deconstruction done by hand.

    Why it comes firstEvery later step asks you to judge an image. Judgement comes from having built the real thing with your own light.
    You pass whenYou can build a clean hero shot from a written brief with one light, one reflector, and no help.
  2. Step 2

    Interrogate: AI as brand strategist

    Builds onThe four-layer deconstruction (S03/02). You bring the analysis; AI stress-tests it.

    Here is my brand research: [paste your pre-activity notes].
Act as a brand strategist. Give me:
1. The brand's five visual codes
2. Its core audience in one sentence
3. Three photographic directions that fit the brand
4. One direction that would be WRONG for this brand, and why.
    ExerciseRun your canvas through the prompt. Keep one insight you missed, reject one generic answer, and write a line on why you rejected it.
    The trapAccepting flattery. Push until the answer has edges; the wrong-direction question is the real test of your analysis.
  3. Step 3

    Visualize: AI as moodboard sketcher

    Builds onLighting language and colour codes (S03/01, S03/05). Prompt writing is art direction vocabulary: describing light to a model is the same language you use to brief an assistant or a client. The Lightplot tool in the studio section turns a physical light position into exactly that wording.

    Product photography mood frame: [product] on [surface/backdrop],
[light quality: soft north window light / hard noon sun / single spot],
[palette: 3 colours from your brand], [lens feel: 100mm macro compression /
35mm environmental], [texture note: condensation, glaze, linen weave].
Mood: [two of your five adjectives].
    ExerciseGenerate three mood frames, then play Spot the Impossible Light: circle every shadow, reflection or highlight that could not physically exist. Rebuild the best frame for real in studio.
    The trapFalling for frames you cannot build. Before a frame enters the moodboard: what light source makes this, do we own it, and what would it cost a client?
  4. Step 4

    Previsualize: AI as production planner

    Builds onEquipment knowledge and the treatment format (S03/09 to S03/11). AI plans logistics; the concept stays yours.

    Here is my moodboard description and 3-shot plan: [paste].
Our studio has: [list the real equipment].
1. Turn this into a shot-by-shot equipment and prop list
2. Flag anything we cannot achieve with this kit
3. Suggest the cheapest workaround for each flagged item.
    ExerciseDraft the production block of your treatment with this prompt, then correct it against the actual studio inventory. Every correction you make is a mark of your expertise.
    The trapLetting AI write your intent. It may fill the equipment list, never the concept statement.
  5. Step 5

    Postproduce: AI in retouch

    Builds onManual cleanup and colour correction (session 10). You must know what honest retouching looks like before automating it.

    After any AI-assisted retouch (cleanup, background extension, fill),
answer before delivery:
1. Is every material still physically believable?
2. Would the client recognise their product on a shelf?
3. What did AI change that I must disclose?
    ExerciseRetouch the same frame twice, once by hand and once AI-assisted. Present both to your team and defend the one you would deliver.
    The trapOver-retouching until the material lies. The product must stay buyable; a texture that cannot exist is a broken promise to the customer.
House rules, at every step
  • Declare all AI use, in the journal and the treatment. This is RMIT academic integrity, and increasingly industry practice.
  • AI output supports concept, planning and cleanup. It is never presented as photography.
  • Do not imitate a named photographer's style.
  • Credit the real campaigns you reference in the moodboard.

ApplyDecide which step you are on today, honestly. Write it in your journal entry; move up one step only when you pass the gate below it.

S03 / 09

Lighting starting points

Three recipes to leave from, not arrive at. Bend every setting toward your brand's visual codes.

Shot 1 / Hero

The brand's signature light

LIGHT
One softbox at 45 degrees high, white card fill opposite.
CAMERA
f/8 to f/11, ISO 100, tripod, tethered if possible.
SET
Seamless backdrop in the brand's owned colour.
WATCH
Edge reflections; move the light, not the product.
Shot 2 / Detail

Texture and material

LIGHT
Hard raking light (bare bulb or snoot) skimming the surface.
CAMERA
Macro or longest lens, f/5.6 to f/8, focus on the material.
SET
Fill the frame; the product's texture is the subject.
WATCH
Dust and fingerprints read ten times larger in macro.
Shot 3 / Lifestyle

The audience's world

LIGHT
Window light or one big soft source; keep it believable.
CAMERA
f/2.8 to f/4 for depth; hands or a model in frame.
SET
Props from the persona sentence, two or three, no more.
WATCH
Styled casual is still styled: check nails, sleeves, clutter.
Tool

Lightplot: test the setup before you rig it

Drag lights around a plot and watch the cast shadows update in real time. Try a position, a height and a modifier before you spend studio time building it, then rig the version you chose. It also writes the setup out as a text prompt, which is the same vocabulary you use to brief an assistant and the same wording AI step 3 asks for.

S03 / 10

Prep and pitfalls

Product prep
  • Clean and dust the product; gloves for glass and metal.
  • Remove stickers and smudges before the first frame, not in Photoshop.
  • Positioning kit: double-sided tape, museum putty, stands, clamps.
  • Shoot for post: leave crop room, keep the light consistent across the set.
Common mistakes
  • Mixed colour temperatures: daylight from the window fighting your tungsten lamp.
  • Props that entertain the photographer but do not belong to the brand.
  • A moodboard in one register and a shoot in another: the pitch test catches this.
  • Over-retouching until the material stops being believable.
S03 / 11

The treatment: one page, four blocks

Due before the next session. This is the shooting script: if it is not on the page, it does not get shot.

Block 1: Concept
  • Brand, product, and the persona sentence.
  • Concept statement in three sentences or fewer.
Block 2: Moodboard
  • The 6 to 9 labeled frames, AI frames declared.
  • The colour story: 3 to 5 swatches with sources.
Block 3: Shot list
  • Hero, detail, lifestyle: one line of intent each.
  • One line of lighting plan per shot.
Block 4: Production
  • Equipment list against the studio inventory.
  • Props and surfaces: what, where from, who brings it.

Before Class / Individual Research

The brand dossier: seven searches

Choose the brand you intend to shoot for the final project. Research it across all seven dimensions and bring the dossier to class as one deck, roughly a page per dimension, images included. This is the raw material for everything you build in studio.

S08 / 01

What to find, where to look, what to bring

Each part takes about twenty minutes. Do them in order: the later searches only make sense once you know who the brand is.

Team worksheet

One page, one team

Team name, members, and who led each of the seven searches, with a line per finding. Fill it in together, attach your slide deck, and bring both to class.

Download the worksheetOne page, one team
  1. Part 01

    Brand

    FindWho they actually are: mission, origin story, price position, and how the brand speaks.

    Where to lookThe About page, founder interviews, packaging copy, and the price list.
    What to bringFive adjectives, the owned colour with its hex code, and one sentence placing the brand against its nearest competitor.
  2. Part 02

    Genre

    FindWhich photographic genres the brand actually lives in: still life, lifestyle, portrait, food, fashion, or a hybrid.

    Where to lookThe last twenty posts on their main account, plus the website gallery.
    What to bringA genre ratio, for example twelve still life, five lifestyle, three portrait out of twenty, and one line on what that ratio reveals about the brand's priorities.
  3. Part 03

    Concept

    FindThe idea the images keep returning to. It is never the product itself.

    Where to lookCampaign pages, seasonal collections, and the captions that introduce them.
    What to bringOne named campaign, its concept in a single sentence, and the strongest frame from it.
  4. Part 04

    Strategy

    FindWhat the brand is trying to win: a launch, a new audience, premiumisation, a seasonal push, or a collaboration.

    Where to lookPress releases, news coverage, collaboration announcements, store openings.
    What to bringOne strategic move from the last twelve months, and the images that carried it.
  5. Part 05

    Social platform

    FindHow the same brand behaves differently on each surface it publishes to.

    Where to lookOne single product, followed across at least three surfaces: the feed, short video, and the product page.
    What to bringThree screenshots of that one product on three platforms, with a note on what changed: crop, aspect ratio, negative space, and how much work the image does alone.
  6. Part 06

    Brief

    FindThe document that must have produced these images. Reverse-engineer it from the evidence.

    Where to lookThe images themselves: repeated crops, recurring props, consistent light, and what never appears.
    What to bringA one-page reconstructed brief: client, deliverables, platforms, tone, mandatories, and what was clearly forbidden.
  7. Part 07

    Storytelling

    FindThe story the brand tells, and the door the audience is invited to walk through.

    Where to lookCampaign films, the sequence of the feed, and the order of images down a landing page.
    What to bringThe story in one sentence, and the three images that tell it, in order.

Part I / 60 min

Lecture: how brand images are governed

Behind every consistent feed sit three documents: a brand kit, a platform plan, and a brief. Learn to read them and you can write them.

S08 / 02

The image ecosystem

A campaign is not one photograph. It is one concept, cut many ways, living on surfaces that each ask for something different.

  1. 01WebsiteThe hero image sets the brand's temperature in one screen; the product page must stay consistent across the whole range.
  2. 02Social feedRead at thumbnail size, mid-scroll, against competitors' images on the same screen.
  3. 03Short videoVertical, motion-first, with safe zones the still frame never worries about.
  4. 04PackagingPrint reproduction, exact colour, and a photograph that survives being held in a hand.
  5. 05Retail and OOHLegible at thirty metres and in three seconds: extreme scale, minimal information.

ApplyFrom dossier part 05, name the surface your brand invests in most. That is where your final campaign has to be strongest.

S08 / 03

Reading a brand kit

A brand kit is the rulebook a client hands you. Most of it is written for designers; one section is written directly for you.

Identity

Logo and clear space

How the mark may sit on an image, and how much empty room it needs. This decides your negative space before you frame.

Type

Typeface and hierarchy

Where headline type will land on your photograph, and therefore which part of the frame must stay quiet.

Colour

Palette with exact values

Hex and print values. Your backdrop, props and grade answer to these numbers, not to your taste.

Photography

The rules for you

Light quality, angle, styling density, models or none, and the forbidden list. Read this section first and last.

ApplyWrite the photography section your brand would have, in five rules. Then write three things this brand must never do.

S08 / 04

Platform grammar

The same concept, recut per surface. Typical shapes, and what each crop demands of the photograph.

SurfaceTypical frameWhat the crop demands
Website hero16:9 wide

Product off-centre, a generous quiet area for headline type, tonal control across a very wide field.

Product page1:1 square

Clean ground, edge-to-edge sharpness, and identical scale and light across every product in the range.

Feed post4:5 vertical

Tighter crop, product large enough to read as a thumbnail, one clear focal point.

Stories and reels9:16 vertical

Safe zones top and bottom, a composition that survives interface overlays and motion.

Packaging and printPrint resolution

Exact colour reproduction, bleed room around the subject, texture that holds at close viewing distance.

Out of homeExtreme ratios

Either enormous scale or extreme close-up. One idea only, legible in three seconds.

ApplyTake one image from your dossier and sketch how you would recut it for three of these surfaces without losing the concept.

S08 / 05

The trend fit test

Trends are borrowed clothes. Run any current trend through four questions before you put it on a brand: a trend adopted against a brand's codes reads as a costume, and audiences notice immediately.

Four questions
  • Does the trend match at least two of the brand's five adjectives?
  • Can we build it with our studio kit, in our timeframe?
  • Does a competitor already own this look so completely that we would look derivative?
  • Does it survive the platform where the image will actually live?
The rule
  • Three or four passes: adopt it, and make it specific to the brand.
  • Two passes: adapt one element only, for example the palette but not the styling.
  • Fewer than two: leave it. Your competitor can have it.
  • The 2026 trend table in the product session is your source material.

ApplyRun two trends through the test for your brand: one you expect to pass, one you expect to fail. Bring both verdicts to the pitch.

S08 / 06

Storytelling structures

Four shapes cover most commercial storytelling. Name the one your brand uses, because it decides what happens inside your frame.

Origin

Where it came from

Craft, material and heritage. The camera looks closely at making. Aesop and Loewe live here.

Ritual

How it is used

A repeated daily moment: hands, morning light, real surfaces. Cocoon and most skincare live here.

Transformation

What it changes

Before and after, texture offered as proof. Rhode's glazed-skin language lives here.

Spectacle

The cultural moment

Scale, surprise, an image built to be shared. Jacquemus and Liquid Death live here.

ApplyName your brand's structure from dossier part 07, then write the single sentence your campaign will tell inside it.

S08 / 07

Reading images back into a brief

Consistency across a feed is never luck. It is a document. Reverse-engineering it teaches you to receive one, and next you write your own.

Evidence to read
  • Repetition: the same angle, prop or surface across months means a mandatory.
  • Absence: what never appears is as instructive as what always does.
  • Consistency of light: one lighting language across a range means a written rule.
  • Crop discipline: identical framing across a product line means a template.
The brief you reconstruct
  • Client and product, in their words rather than yours.
  • Deliverables: how many images, in which ratios, for which surfaces.
  • Tone: the five adjectives again, now written as instructions.
  • Mandatories and prohibitions: what must appear, and what may never.

ApplyCompare your reconstructed brief with a classmate's for a different brand. Where the two briefs differ most is where the brands differ most.

Part II / 105 min

Studio: build the campaign

Turn the dossier into the two documents your final project runs on: a brand kit you can shoot to, and a campaign board that holds across three surfaces.

S08 / 08

Build the brand kit

One page, for your chosen brand, written so that another photographer could shoot it correctly without asking you a single question.

Include
  • Palette: three to five colours with hex codes, pulled from the real brand.
  • Five adjectives, and the story structure you named.
  • Photography rules: light quality, angle, styling density, and depth.
  • Three prohibitions: what this brand must never look like.
Test it
  • Hand your kit to another team without naming the brand.
  • Ask them to describe the photograph they would take from it.
  • If their description fits the brand, the kit works. If not, your rules are too vague.
